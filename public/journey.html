From the team notebook

How we got started.

We began with a school-team waitlist, a new community team, and a lot to learn about FTC robotics.

AN ENCOURAGING START

A first-year Innovation Award.

Our team earned an Innovation Award in its first year. It gave us encouragement to keep developing our ideas and learning new skills.

The practical work around that milestone is recorded in our first-season portfolio: waiting for parts, learning to program, attending scrimmages, and revising the robot.

FIRST-SEASON ARCHIVE

One change at a time.

SEPTEMBER

Start the club. Learn the basics.

While waiting for robot parts, we practiced Blockly programming and learned about robot components and FTC OpModes.

OCTOBER

Get a first robot running.

We assembled the initial robot, wrote our first OpMode and a basic TeleOp program, and planned an outreach event.

NOVEMBER

Try it around other teams.

We attended our first scrimmage, switched to mecanum wheels, held an outreach event, and wrote our first autonomous program. We also learned about encoders and ticks.

DECEMBER

Add reach. Work on balance.

We added the Viper slide, developed the 3D-printed Cyber Claw, and used repurposed hitches to adjust weight distribution.

LATER IN THE SEASON

Keep testing.

We improved autonomous operation and took part in a second scrimmage.

This timeline follows the first-season portfolio. Calendar years are not supplied in that record.

THE WORK CONTINUES

A team built around learning.

Our team brings together three high school students and two middle school students. Designing, building, and programming give us different ways to contribute and plenty of reasons to learn from each other.
